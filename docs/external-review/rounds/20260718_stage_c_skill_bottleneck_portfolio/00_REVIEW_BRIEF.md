# Stage C Skill-Bottleneck Portfolio Review Brief

## Purpose

Stage C has ended validly as `SUPPORT_H2_SKILL_LIMIT`. This round does not
rescue F0/F1 or select a unique successor. It expands and stress-tests a plural
algorithm portfolio for the upstream question exposed by the result:

> How can one shared HMASD-derived algorithm learn executable, reusable skills
> when team membership and useful skill lifetime both vary, without encoding
> task semantics in the intrinsic reward?

The two divergent reviewers independently propose or reject two to four causal
explanations and architectural replacements. The controller will compare them
against the repository. The convergent reviewer will rank the resulting
portfolio and recommend a next serialized evidence source, not a uniquely legal
research direction.

## Binding evidence

1. The Stage A dynamic-roster carrier is valid and reachable.
2. The Stage B anonymous recurrent primitive-action controller is valid and
   learns the task: final deterministic `P/S/U=1/0.998210/0.999105`, stochastic
   `U=0.986654`.
3. The paired Stage C run is implementation-valid at exactly 320,000 transitions
   per arm. Both F0 and F1 finish at `P/S/U=0/1/0.5`; paired F1-minus-F0 utility
   CI95 is `[0,0,0]`.
4. F1 is not inert. It has natural working-versus-initial prefix-TV CI95
   `[0.047562,0.050023,0.052577]`, positive directional composition shift, and
   forced-audit `rho=0.070304` with CI95
   `[0.058321,0.070304,0.111164]`.
5. Neither arm has executable naturally used skills. The registered Stage C
   disposition therefore stops that route without selector rescue, timing read,
   retuning or a new module.
6. Original HMASD remains a positive fixed-`N` Alice--Bob source anchor. It uses
   skill-conditioned low control plus environment-agnostic `q_d/q_D` mutual-
   information rewards, but it does not by itself solve dynamic membership or
   asynchronous lifetime.
7. R29 and R31--R33 already retire direct action-information reward,
   observational/forced-effect reward, direct individual-effect policy gradient
   and intervention-scored roster complementarity under their exact contracts.
   They may be used diagnostically but not renamed or tuned back into service.

## Final capability and invariants

The final target is one shared policy that handles episode-internal anonymous
join, temporary leave, rejoin and terminal leave while allowing heterogeneous
realized skill lifetimes. It must retain:

- active-only execution and survivor recurrent continuity;
- exact probability/replay, duration-aware credit and strict checkpoint state;
- decentralized low execution with no identity, roster slot, future membership
  or task-specific oracle;
- a clean separation between external task reward and environment-agnostic
  intrinsic skill learning;
- original HMASD semantics where they remain defensible, rather than a growing
  stack of independent modules.

Task state fields, role labels, progress, contacts, success predicates and
external reward may not be relabeled as intrinsic reward. A proposal that needs
such inputs is outside the portfolio.

## Starting causal portfolio

These are starting hypotheses, not selected routes.

### H2-A — missing environment-agnostic skill-learning pressure

The dynamic Stage C trainer supplies shared terminal task credit but no active
intrinsic objective that makes `z_i` predict reusable individual behavior. F1
can move label probabilities while the low policy has no reason to make labels
executable. A valid replacement may need to restore or reformulate the original
HMASD semantic pressure for variable membership and mixed-age segments.

### H2-B — task alignment and credit mismatch

Small forced skill effects exist, but the high assignment objective cannot
select effects aligned with useful persistent/reactive roles from terminal team
credit. The problem may be joint skill credit or representation, not low-policy
capacity. Direct effect maximization and R33 complementarity are retired; a new
route must be structurally different.

### H2-C — fixed skill bottleneck is the wrong dynamic-team interface

`pi_low(a_i | o_i,z_i)` may be too restrictive when anonymous membership and
mixed-age context change. A replacement could alter how a reusable skill is
conditioned or composed while preserving decentralized execution and a clear
skill bottleneck. It must replace an existing component rather than append a
graph, field, slot, team latent or second controller by default.

### H2-D — hierarchy is unnecessary or unidentified on this substrate

The direct Stage B controller may be the correct ordinary-MARL explanation for
the toy. Stage C may reveal that the testbed cannot identify useful reusable
skills even though it identifies dynamic-team control. A defensible outcome is
to redesign the evidence substrate or stop this hierarchy line, not manufacture
role labels or shaping.

Reviewers may merge, split or replace these hypotheses, but the final portfolio
must contain two to four genuinely distinct causal mechanisms.

## Replacement discipline

Every candidate must state:

- what current component it retains, deletes and replaces;
- how data, gradients, clocks, masks and segment ownership flow;
- how its intrinsic signal remains environment-agnostic;
- why it is not standard MARL plus a scheduler or a renamed retired route;
- the observation that would distinguish it from at least one other candidate;
- its smallest evidence source and the branches that update the whole portfolio.

Do not propose a blind module menu. Prefer one architectural simplification or
replacement with a coherent causal story.

## Review outcome

This round produces a ranked portfolio and a serialized evidence sequence. It
does not authorize implementation, training, a Stage C rerun, learned timing,
reward shaping or UAV-scale claims. A stop is valid only if the evidence
exhausts the whole portfolio, not merely one candidate.
