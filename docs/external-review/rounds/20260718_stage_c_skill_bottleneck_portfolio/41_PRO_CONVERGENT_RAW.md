1. Evidence validity

Stage B establishes access, not hierarchy invalidity. The anonymous recurrent primitive-action controller learned the exact dynamic-roster task to near perfection under both deterministic and stochastic evaluation. This establishes that the carrier, observations, active-set representation, recurrent continuity, sparse terminal reward, and anonymous membership changes are jointly learnable on this substrate. It also supplies a strong ordinary-MARL null. It does not establish that every ordinary-MARL formulation would work, because the Stage B instrument has a powerful primitive-time autoregressive frontier with earlier-action counts. Nor does it establish that hierarchy is unnecessary for transfer, reusable macro-behavior, longer commitments, sample efficiency, or the final UAV-scale capability. Stage B was an access gate, not a capacity- and factorization-matched proof against every hierarchy.

Stage C establishes failure of the registered F0/F1 route and a skill-execution bottleneck, but not its cause. The paired run is implementation-valid, the selector is not dead, and F1 changes later high-level distributions: its prefix-TV confidence interval is clearly positive. However, the directional-composition interval is only approximately [0.0083, 0.0129], below the registered lower-bound threshold of 0.02; both arms end at P/S/U=0/1/0.5; the utility contrast is exactly zero; and the forced-skill separation is orders of magnitude below the executable-skill requirement, with F1 rho≈0.0703 versus a required lower confidence bound above 1. Detectable dependence on z is therefore not equivalent to a stable behavior skill. The valid conclusion is “the current hierarchy failed to form and naturally use executable skills,” not “the low actor ignores z,” “the selector is broken,” “credit is definitely wrong,” or “hierarchy is structurally impossible.”

The Gemini review overclaims in three places. First, “the low-level policy ignores the labels” is contradicted by the positive forced dependence; the dependence is weak or context-sensitive, not absent. Second, calling the dynamic q_d/q_D route a direct port of a “proven” cause treats the original HMASD result as an isolating ablation that it was not. Third, its proposed duration scaling risks rewarding long survival rather than skill semantics. Its “structural failure” language should be narrowed to failure under this exact architecture, objective, substrate, and budget.

The Open Pro review is more disciplined, but “a statistical skill exists” is still too strong. Stage C establishes a small z-conditioned effect, not cross-agent, cross-age, or cross-membership semantic stability. Its notation p(behavior | z, age) is also unsafe if age is allowed to predict the label: age and duration must be nuisance variables used for matching or stratified audit, not convenient decoders of z.

Original HMASD is a positive source anchor, not a causal certificate. It shows that fixed-N skill-conditioned execution combined with environment-agnostic individual/team discriminator machinery can participate in a successful Alice–Bob system. It does not show that q_d or q_D alone caused success, that their one-step form survives asynchronous lifetimes, or that a sampled team latent remains actionable under variable membership. The repository’s own evidence already warns that a team code can be decorative and that q_D is inadmissible before a separate actionability gate.

R29 and R31–R33 are exact-contract negative evidence. They exclude revival of direct action-information reward, observational or forced-effect reward, direct individual-effect policy gradients, and intervention-scored roster complementarity under their registered definitions. They do not prove that all information-theoretic process semantics, all SMDP credit, or all cooperative representations are impossible. Their statistics may remain diagnostic, but their failed objectives cannot be renamed, softened, or retuned back into service.

The controller synthesis is substantially correct, with two qualifications. Its proposed checkpoint audit can establish causal dependence only where z is actually intervened on under the same snapshot; natural clustering alone is observational. Also, the proposed outcome “stable skills exist and add no transfer or commitment advantage over Stage B” exceeds what the stated audit can determine unless a matched direct checkpoint and existing held-out transfer evaluations are already part of the serialized evidence. The audit can strengthen C3 as a null, but cannot by itself establish final-target dominance by direct control.

2. Portfolio judgment

My evidence-weighted ranking is:

1. C3 — hierarchy-null active-set control. This is the leading explanation of the current substrate. Stage B solves the task almost perfectly; Stage C adds a discrete skill bottleneck, high policy, event-time credit, and applied-prefix machinery without producing executable skills or utility. C3 therefore has the strongest direct empirical support for the proposition that this short testbed does not require explicit hierarchy. This is a ranking of the hypothesis, not a recommendation to abandon the final reusable-skill objective.

2. C1 — segment-owned environment-agnostic skill semantics. C1 is the strongest live hierarchical explanation. The Stage C low path receives no active objective whose job is to make z denote a persistent, reusable behavior process. That absence makes C1 plausible and closely connected to the original HMASD functional intent. Its confidence should nevertheless be moderate, not high: the evidence has not isolated missing semantic pressure from executor capacity, event-context aliasing, or the possibility that arbitrary diversity would remain task-irrelevant.

3. C2 — minimal event-context execution interface. C2 is viable but currently least supported. The low actor already has recurrence, current observation, and z; no existing analysis shows that failures concentrate at new-skill, rejoin, mixed-age, or membership-event boundaries. A new event context could simply add capacity, reveal schedule shortcuts, or reproduce information a matched recurrent policy can infer. C2 should rise only if the checkpoint audit localizes otherwise-real z dependence to those boundaries.

The SMDP credit branch is correctly conditional. Credit cannot select stable useful skills until a stable executor exists. A positive forced rho, nonuniform skill occupancy, or a few distinguishable trajectories is insufficient. Activation requires all of the following: reproducible skill-conditioned behavior; stability across anonymous lifecycles, age, and active-N; meaningful overlap with naturally visited segments; and evidence that the high policy fails to select those already-existing semantics under external return. Until then, a credit estimator would be trying to assign value to an unidentified object.

C1 and C2 should remain competing explanations during diagnosis. Combining a new semantic objective with a new event-context input in the first comparison would destroy causal attribution. C3 remains the mandatory null throughout.

3. Replacement and invariants audit
C1 — segment-owned semantic pressure

Retain: the schema-3 lifecycle runtime; anonymous active-only execution; discrete z; combined categorical KEEP/SET; the existing high/event and low/physical policy factors; exact recorded order, masks, and behavior likelihoods; survivor recurrent continuity; task-only high return; and the separation between external and intrinsic reward.

Delete or replace: replace the implicit training assumption that terminal external reward alone will give the skill token reusable semantics. C1 is admissible as one change to the low-path training contract, not as q_d + q_D + team latent + context + scheduler. The external task objective is not deleted. The default team latent and q_D remain absent.

Probability: a discriminator or process encoder contributes no actor likelihood of its own. High-policy replay remains the exact event-frontier product over stored combined actions and masks. Low-policy replay remains the exact primitive-action probability. External membership events, temporary leave, rejoin, and exogenous opportunity timing acquire no actor ratio.

Gradient: classifier likelihood or contrastive loss trains the semantic estimator. A detached intrinsic score may train the low actor through its physical-time policy return. It must not silently enter the high/event advantage, train the KEEP/SET selector, or backpropagate classifier loss through the sampled discrete z. High assignment remains task-credit-only unless the separately conditional credit branch is later activated. This preserves the causal separation between “make a selected skill executable” and “select a useful skill.”

Physical and event time: the high trace retains owner-specific gamma^Delta event credit. Intrinsic semantic credit belongs to active primitive execution. It should be normalized over active steps or fixed active-time windows rather than multiplied by realized duration, because raw segment-level accumulation would reward surviving longer and confound skill meaning with lifetime. Silent primitive steps still create no high action.

Segment ownership: a segment belongs to one focal lifecycle and one active skill. SET closes the old skill segment and starts the new one. Another member’s join, leave, or policy opportunity does not terminate a survivor’s segment. Temporary leave censors the active fragment and freezes the lifecycle; inactive time is excluded. Rejoin restores hidden state, skill, and age, but opens a new collected chunk from the restored state. Terminal leave ends ownership. PPO-update boundaries may preserve simulator state but must split learning data by policy version; no process sample crosses versions.

Masks and checkpoints: only active rows are scored; padding, absence, and routing keys are masked and unavailable to the model. Any later semantic mechanism would have to add its estimator, optimizer, normalization, split, and RNG state to the already fail-closed checkpoint contract, while preserving lifecycle, opportunity, frontier-order, open-trace, environment, and policy-version state.

C2 — minimal event-context execution

Retain: discrete skills, high KEEP/SET, all event probabilities, external task credit, masks, lifecycle ownership, recurrence, and checkpoint exactness.

Delete or replace: replace only the strict low interface pi_low(a_i | o_i, z_i) with an explicitly bounded focal event context. This would supersede the present “no new low-level input” contract and therefore must be treated as a genuine replacement, not an innocuous implementation detail. It cannot be stacked with C1 in its first causal comparison.

The proposed context remains underspecified. To be admissible it must name one smallest runtime-owned, task-agnostic distinction that the current low path cannot reliably recover—for example, a registered new-versus-resumed execution boundary. It may not include identity, roster position, future membership, task phase, progress, role, reward, or a learned team controller. Skill age is particularly dangerous: it may be useful policy context, but it can also turn the interface into a schedule decoder. Any later test would need a matched-capacity null and explicit evidence that the gain comes from event disambiguation rather than additional width or privileged timing.

Probability and replay: the primitive action likelihood would be conditioned on the exact stored event context used at collection. High likelihoods remain unchanged. No membership event acquires a policy probability.

Gradient: only the low execution path receives gradients through this additional conditioning. The context cannot provide a differentiable bypass from global state or the high controller into primitive actions.

Time, masks, segments, and checkpoints: all existing lifecycle, active-only, censoring, SET, rejoin, terminal, and policy-version boundaries remain unchanged. Any non-derivable context state must be versioned and restored exactly; a value reconstructed differently at replay would invalidate the probability contract.

C3 — hierarchy-null direct control

Retain: the anonymous active-lifecycle table; active-only permutation-compatible representation; survivor recurrent continuity; typed membership transactions; direct primitive-time autoregressive ordering; centralized value context; external reward; and exact physical-time PPO.

Delete or replace: remove high assignment, discrete skill conditioning, KEEP/SET, macro/event actor likelihoods, semantic segments, and high SMDP GAE. Replace the hierarchy with the Stage B class of direct active-set recurrent primitive policy.

Probability: the only learned actor factors are primitive-action probabilities in the recorded physical-step frontier. Any uniform external order remains a policy-independent constant. Membership and routing transactions have no action probability.

Gradient and time: external task return trains the direct actor and critic in physical time. There is no intrinsic skill gradient and no owner-specific skill segment. Event metadata remains infrastructure, not a learned scheduler action.

Masks and checkpoints: active-only masks and survivor continuity remain mandatory. The checkpoint can omit skill, opportunity, and open high-trace state only because those objects no longer exist, but must still preserve direct recurrent state, critic, optimizer, normalizer, environment snapshot, active presentation, membership transaction, and all RNG state needed for exact continuation.

C3 does not satisfy “heterogeneous skill lifetime” by reinterpretation: it rejects the need for an explicit skill-lifetime object. Its burden is to cover the final behavioral capability without one.

Conditional SMDP credit

If activated, this branch retains a demonstrated executor and replaces only the high assignment return estimator. It must use the stored high behavior likelihood, focal owner boundaries, physical duration Delta, and gamma^Delta; other members’ events do not advance the owner’s trace. Its gradient belongs to the high assignment policy, not the low executor. A clean causal read may require holding the executor fixed or otherwise preventing simultaneous semantic drift. Effect scores, forced-effect rewards, individual-effect gradients, role labels, and R31–R33 estimators remain excluded.

q_D should not be live in C1. The current event architecture deliberately removed a sampled team latent, bridge, default q_D, and team-code reward. Earlier evidence also shows that a team latent can be behaviorally decorative. A roster-pooled classifier with no sampled, actionable team variable would merely create a team-looking statistic; it would not reconstruct HMASD’s cooperative mechanism. Team-level semantics can be reconsidered only after a separately justified, roster-invariant coordination variable has a demonstrated causal path into assignment and behavior. Until then, C1 should be an individual focal-process hypothesis, with cooperative usefulness judged externally through composition and task return.

Even a shortcut-resistant C1 pass would establish only that skills exist and generalize as behavior processes. It would not establish that the skills are useful, complementary, or superior to ordinary MARL.

5. Discriminating evidence

Modify and accept the proposed existing-checkpoint audit. It is the correct next evidence class, but its causal and inferential boundaries must be tightened.

The smallest next source is one same-snapshot, nuisance-matched, existing-checkpoint skill-semantics audit using the serialized Stage C final checkpoints, stored natural ledger, and already registered forced branches. No training, reward, parameter sweep, new module, or new task is needed.

The audit should combine three reads:

Direct intervention read. For the same focal snapshot, observation, recurrent state, legal action support, exogenous ledger, and boundary state, intervene only on z. Compare exact primitive action distributions and paired fixed-active-time trajectory distributions. Where stochastic branches are required, use common exogenous randomness and separate policy-action randomness appropriately. Natural label correlations are not a substitute for this intervention.

Stability read. Evaluate whether the resulting generic action/process signatures preserve their relative meaning across held-out lifecycle instances, active-age bins, join/rejoin or ordinary-execution strata, and active-N. Age, duration, and membership state are matching variables, not semantic features. Include context-only, shuffled-label, skill-prior, age-matched, and duration-matched nulls.

Natural-overlap read. Determine whether naturally assigned segments bearing z occupy the same process region as the forced z branches. A skill that exists only when forcibly imposed is not a naturally executable skill. Natural skill frequency must not dominate the comparison.

Metrics, splits, minimum support, and interpretation thresholds must be frozen before running this reanalysis. Unsupported strata produce UNDERPOWERED, not a favorable merge of categories. R29/R31-style effect statistics remain instruments only.

The portfolio-wide outcomes are:

A. No material same-snapshot dependence on z.
C3 strengthens and remains first. C1 also gains relative support as the best hierarchical repair hypothesis, but missing semantic pressure is still not proved. C2 weakens unless the null is specifically confined to event-transition strata. The SMDP credit branch closes because there is no stable object to credit.

B. Dependence exists, but it changes, disappears, or reverses with skill age, join/rejoin, or membership events.
C2 becomes the leading hierarchical explanation and may overtake C1. C1 remains possible because semantic pressure could also improve invariance, but it is no longer the uniquely natural cause. C3 remains a strong null. Credit stays premature.

C. Stable forced semantics exist, but natural segments do not express or occupy them.
The strong version of C1 is downweighted: the executor can express stable z-conditioned modes without a new semantic objective. C2 weakens unless the forced/natural mismatch is localized to event boundaries. The SMDP assignment/credit branch opens, subject to a later clean test of whether the failure is selection, credit, or exploration. C3 remains competitive because the hierarchy still contributes no utility.

D. Stable forced semantics and natural overlap both exist, but Stage C utility remains zero.
C1 and broad C2 are substantially downweighted. The conditional credit/alignment branch gains support, but this outcome also strengthens C3: the substrate may contain distinguishable skills that have no useful reason to exist. The audit must not call this a credit success; it only identifies credit or task alignment as the next unresolved edge.

E. Apparent stability vanishes against duration-, age-, or context-matched nulls.
Treat the apparent skill as a shortcut. C1 rises, C2 does not receive credit for schedule decoding, C3 rises, and SMDP credit remains closed.

F. The required strata or serialized fields are insufficient.
Return UNDERPOWERED without causal reweighting beyond preserving C3 as the current empirical leader. Do not replace missing evidence with a bespoke environment or post-hoc threshold.

One controller outcome should be removed from this audit: it cannot establish that stable skills lack transfer or commitment advantage over Stage B unless those matched held-out evaluations already exist. Distinct hierarchy value is a later question.

6. Ordinary-MARL objection

The strongest reduction is not merely “use a bigger flat network.” It is:

Use the Stage B class of anonymous, active-set, primitive-time autoregressive recurrent policy with the same lifecycle continuity, active masks, membership observations, centralized value information, and recorded external ordering. Let recurrent state internalize persistence and event history. Treat exogenous opportunities, event flags, or skill age as ordinary observations only when a hierarchy is allowed the same information.

On the present testbed this policy already produces the persistent-versus-reactive composition directly, without z, KEEP/SET, macro GAE, or an intrinsic skill objective. Therefore:

C1 can reduce to auxiliary diversity regularization unless its learned behaviors are reusable and composable.

C2 can reduce to an extra input or capacity increase unless a matched flat controller cannot use the same context.

A discrete skill selector can reduce to an exogenous scheduler or finite-state mixture unless z creates persistent behavior that survives transfer and recomposition.

Masking and survivor recurrence already solve variable membership mechanically; they are not hierarchy contributions.

Later evidence would demonstrate distinct value only if a hierarchy, against a capacity-, information-, exposure-, and optimization-matched direct policy, provides a material advantage on at least one load-bearing dimension: generalization to unseen roster schedules or active counts; robustness across substantially different realized lifetimes; faster learning under sparse delayed reward; transfer of a frozen or reusable low-level skill set; recomposition into new commitments; or stable long-horizon coordination that the direct recurrent policy cannot maintain. Equal return, interpretable labels, positive mutual information, or longer nominal commitments are insufficient.

That comparison belongs after executable semantics are established. The current checkpoint audit cannot adjudicate it.

7. Parked ideas

Continuous skill composition should be recorded but not promoted. Replacing categorical skills and KEEP/SET with an end-to-end continuous vector currently removes the exact discrete behavior probability, commitment, and lifetime contract; may collapse into a deeper flat recurrent network; and supplies no intrinsic reason for the vector to represent reusable behavior. It becomes coherent only if the discrete bottleneck itself is later contradicted and the proposal specifies a stochastic density or other exact learning contract, persistence semantics, checkpoint state, and a matched flat-network reduction.

A future actionable team latent and q_D should remain parked. It requires a demonstrated team-code-to-assignment and team-code-to-behavior path before any discriminator reward. A decorative pooled team embedding is specifically excluded by prior evidence.

Learned event timing, hazard, or point-process termination remains parked. Stage C did not satisfy the timing-read prerequisites, and learned timing would introduce survival, intensity, and censoring probabilities beyond the current contract.

A fixed-executor assignment diagnostic from the Open review is useful only after the checkpoint audit establishes stable executable skills. It could later separate executor drift from high-level assignment, but running it now would assume the object whose existence is disputed.

Longer process windows, cross-age stability, and cross-agent reuse metrics are useful diagnostic ideas and should be preserved. They must not become reward terms or task-specific role definitions.

Intrinsic-only then intrinsic-plus-task training, proposed by Gemini, is a possible later promotion sequence, not the next source. It is premature before the existing data establish whether the current executor has latent capacity, event aliasing, or stable forced semantics.

Duration-scaled mutual-information reward should be recorded as rejected under the current contract rather than kept as a default option: without a derivation that removes survival bias, it rewards lifetime and invites age shortcuts.

The SMDP credit branch is not a general parked idea; it is a dormant live branch with a precise activation condition.

8. Recommendation boundary

Recommendation: REANALYSIS — authorize exactly one serialized evidence source, the modified existing-checkpoint causal/nuisance skill-semantics audit described above.

This recommendation does not reopen Stage C, change its SUPPORT_H2_SKILL_LIMIT disposition, authorize training, add a discriminator, modify the low interface, activate credit, or promote a successor. Serialization orders information acquisition; it does not make C1, C2, or C3 the uniquely legal research direction.

A later implementation comparison would be justified only by a specific audit result:

a genuine z-null, together with evidence that the actor can express alternative modes, would justify considering one C1 semantic-objective intervention;

event-local or age-local instability would justify considering one minimal C2 interface replacement;

stable cross-context semantics plus natural assignment failure would justify activation of the SMDP credit branch;

stable semantics with no natural usefulness would strengthen C3 and require a separate decision about whether the substrate can identify hierarchical value.

None of those outcomes authorizes implementation automatically; each would still require its own registered comparator, probability contract, thresholds, stop branches, and authorization.

The current testbed route is already stopped. The whole portfolio is not yet exhausted, because C1 and C2 remain causally unidentified and the checkpoint evidence has not tested final-target hierarchy value.

The whole portfolio would be exhausted only after evidence jointly establishes that:

a shortcut-resistant process-semantic route cannot produce stable executable skills;

a minimal event-context replacement does not repair any localized instability;

no stable executor exists that can activate a defensible SMDP credit branch; and

a matched direct active-set controller covers the final variable-membership capability without a material scaling, transfer, sample-efficiency, or long-horizon commitment deficit.

Conversely, later integration would require both reproducible environment-agnostic skill semantics and a demonstrated advantage over the matched direct-policy null. A skill-existence diagnostic alone cannot satisfy that boundary.
