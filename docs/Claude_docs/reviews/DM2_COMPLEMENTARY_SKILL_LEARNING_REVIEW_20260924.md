## Verdict

The bookkeeping in this direction is exemplary and every headline number in NOTES reproduces exactly from the raw panels, but the science is currently answering the wrong question with instruments that cannot detect the right answer. Two facts, both recoverable from the retained `training.jsonl` rows and never stated in NOTES or RESEARCH, dominate everything else: (i) the high-level coordinator's PPO loss is arithmetically dominated by the entropy bonus (λ_h = 0.07 applied to the sum of seven categorical entropies), so the "learned" high-level distribution sits at 95.2 % (team) and 99.6 % (individual, over the mixture) of maximum entropy after 45 updates — the learned selector is, to first order, a uniform sampler; and (ii) both skill discriminators are at chance (implied log q ≈ −1.67 / −1.75 against ln(1/6) = −1.79; accuracies 0.22–0.33 against 0.167, flat over training), so the "skill discovery" reward is a near-constant negative offset and the six labels are barely distinguishable behaviours. Under those two facts the B04 "bank gain vs deployment loss" decomposition is not evidence about complementary skills at all: M ≈ U-plus-a-tilt explains a +0.0354 J single-seed difference that is the same size as the B01→B03 sign reversal, and "own argmax < uniform" (negative in users in 4/4 learned banks) is the expected consequence of taking the argmax of a nearly flat distribution — it removes the across-agent label diversity that uniform sampling supplies. The pending 0-fit O/S/R comparison is cheap and correctly designed as an identity-controlled evaluator, but as specified (J-only) it will most likely return S ≈ R > O, which the entropy telemetry already predicts; its real value lies in the label-usage reading it does not yet compute. The line is not publishable on its own (n = 1 per arm, no positive effect of the hierarchy over random labels, discriminators at chance) and, as it stands, would undermine rather than support a team-size-transfer paper; the uniform/U control is nonetheless exactly the ablation such a paper needs, and a sharpened-selector version of it is the one experiment worth buying next.

## 2 Code and protocol findings

**F1 — Coordinator loss is dominated by the entropy bonus; the learned high level is near-uniform. Severity: blocker (for the interpretation of B04 and of "learned assignment").** `hmasd/agent.py` `update_coordinator_d2` builds `total_entropy_per_sample = team_entropy + Σ_i agent_entropy_i` (≈ lines 6145–6148), normalises all advantages globally (≈ 6160–6164) and adds `entropy_loss = −lambda_h · entropy` with `lambda_h = 0.07` from `configs/config_1.py:165` (comment at :197: "higher to encourage exploration"). With seven categorical heads of ≤ ln 6 = 1.79 nats each, the bonus is worth up to 0.88 per sample, while the clipped-surrogate policy loss with unit-variance advantages is O(0.03). Verified from `b04_m/training.jsonl` [verified]: rollout 45, policy −0.0274 + value 0.202 − 0.07 × (1.706 + 6 × 1.785) = −0.694 vs logged `coordinator_loss` −0.6944; rollout 12, 0.0078 + 0.800 − 0.07 × (1.720 + 6 × 1.773) = −0.0574 vs logged −0.0577. Consequences: `team_skill_entropy` ends at 1.706 (95.2 % of ln 6) and `agent_skill_entropy` at 1.785 (99.6 %); for the individual heads the entropy is of μ = 0.9π + 0.1/6 (`b01/learning.py:39-58`), and a deterministic π would give H(μ) = 0.42, so π itself is near-uniform. The same pattern holds in every M-type arm of B01–B03 (team 1.69–1.76, individual 1.78–1.79). The 675 coordinator optimizer steps are real but mostly fit the value head (value loss 1.94 → 0.20); the policy has essentially not moved off uniform. Therefore B04 "M" is "uniform + a small state-dependent tilt + autoregressive correlation", and Δ_uniform = +0.0354 cannot be read as the value of learned assignment. This is a project default, not a DM choice, but the DM's own telemetry contained it from B01 rollout 1 onward.

**F2 — "Own" deployment is the argmax of a near-flat distribution. Severity: major.** `b01/runner.py:200-218` calls `assign_partial_batch(..., deterministic=True)`; `hmasd/networks.py:1072` and `:1099` take `argmax` for the team token and each autoregressive individual token. With per-state logits whose softmax is within 0.08 nats KL of uniform, the argmax is decided by small bias terms and is likely near-constant across states and agents [inferred]: the training team-label marginal already tilts to labels 5 and 3 (0.205 / 0.191 vs 0.137 min [verified], `b04/runner.py:455-457`). Deploying one label to all six UAVs for 500 steps removes exactly the across-agent diversity that independent uniform draws supply, which is the simplest explanation of the 4/4 negative own−uniform users effect in §1. The B01–B04 panels store only a digest of the selected labels (`b03/runner.py:149,170-172,217`), so this cannot be confirmed from main; B05 stores the label arrays and factor log-probabilities (`b05/runner.py:580-586, 700-711`) and can confirm it at zero cost. Train/deploy mismatch is real but secondary: training samples labels and Gaussian actions, evaluation uses argmax labels and mean actions; sampling labels (S) restores only the label half.

**F3 — Discriminator reward is a near-constant at chance level. Severity: major.** `hmasd/agent.py:4400-4447`: team component = 0.5 × λ_D(0.05) × clip(log q(Z | s'), ±2), individual = 0.5 × λ_d(0.02) × clip(log q(z_i | o_i'), ±2), no prior correction (`use_prior_corrected_intrinsic = false`). Logged means [verified]: `avg_team_disc_comp` −0.0408 to −0.0447 in every arm and rollout ⇒ mean log q ≈ −1.67 (q ≈ 0.19; chance 0.167); `avg_ind_disc_comp` −0.0173 to −0.0176 ⇒ log q ≈ −1.75 (q ≈ 0.17). `discriminator_team_accuracy` 0.24–0.38 and `individual` 0.28–0.38, not improving over 45 rollouts (B04 M team 0.375 → 0.317; U 0.282 → 0.219). The environment component is +0.07/step, so the intrinsic term is a −0.06/step offset that carries almost no label information. Implications: (a) the six "skills" are not distinguishable behaviours, so no complementarity test on them can succeed; (b) B02's T−M result is best explained as an optimisation artefact of removing a constant offset (return scale, ValueNorm, advantage normalisation; T's action entropy and saturation rose), not as evidence that the discovery objective helps — the RESEARCH background sentence "removing low discriminator rewards loses native service" over-reads an n = 1 confound.

**F4 — Single seed per arm throughout. Severity: major (constitution §8.1).** Every batch has one training instance per arm; "27/32 worlds" is nested deployment variation. Between-world SD of Δ_uniform is 0.0345 (paired t = 5.81 across worlds) [verified], but B03 already demonstrated that a world-level t = 4.05 effect (B01 G−D uniform) reverses under one new training trajectory. The notebook states this limitation correctly; the RESEARCH DM2 row and background paragraphs nonetheless carry the numbers as findings.

**F5 — B01 own and uniform panels use different worlds. Severity: minor (acknowledged).** `b01/runner.py:51-53` (own final base 1700100, uniform 1700200). B02–B04 fixed this.

**F6 — U is a fair "no learning" control for the label law, not for the AR structure. Severity: minor.** `b04/learning.py:111-157` replaces the decoder output with zero logits, so U's team and individual labels are independent uniform draws; M's are autoregressively correlated. U's coordinator forward still runs (549 inference calls) and its discriminators still train on uniform labels (675/2700 steps). The declared "matched transitions" claim holds; the comparison conflates learned tilt with AR correlation, which the DM half-notes ("labels can convey global information differently").

**F7 — High-level training budget is structurally small. Severity: minor.** Per rollout the coordinator sees 800 decisions (16 lanes × 50 renewals) and takes 15 steps (`ppo_epochs = 15`, one 800-row batch since `coordinator_batch_size = 1280 > 800`); 675 steps total on 36 000 decisions over a 6⁷ = 280 k joint label space, versus 101 250 low-level steps. Combined with F1 this makes "high level undertrained" true but not the binding constraint; the entropy bonus is.

**F8 — Marginal label entropies are not the right collapse diagnostic. Severity: minor.** NOTES cites "final behaviour entropies 1.786 near log 6" (occupancy over sampled labels, `b04/runner.py:592-594`). Because sampled individual labels come from μ with a 0.1 uniform floor and the team policy is entropy-pinned, near-max marginal entropy is guaranteed and says nothing about state-conditional structure. The conditional entropies in `native_losses` are the informative quantities and show the opposite problem: no structure at all.

**F9 — B05 evaluator is sound but computes no label-usage statistics. Severity: minor (blocking for information value).** `b05/runner.py:520-569`: O = `deterministic=True`, S = `deterministic=False` under a private Torch stream (individual tokens sampled from μ, team from π — consistent with training), R = independent PCG64 with R0 = B04 seed 262624105 as an identity control (`:43,47,1051-1066`). `aggregate_panels` (`:856-899`) reports only J/users/coverage/quality/height. Given F1, the predicted outcome is S ≈ R within deployment noise (SE over 32 worlds ≈ 0.006–0.01 J for a between-rule difference) and S > O by roughly the B04 A_M magnitude; the J-only reading would then leave the mechanism unstated. The trajectories saved at `:580-586` make a histogram/entropy reading free.

**F10 — Action saturation 0.58 at deployment.** 58 % of raw mean-action coordinates lie outside [−1, 1] (`b01/runner.py:253`; panel `raw_saturation_fraction` [verified]); the low-level policy is largely bang-bang, which limits how much any label can modulate behaviour. Observation, not a defect.

Verified integrity items that pass: identity J = 0.7C + 0.3Q − H; Γ = Δ + A_M world-by-world; initial uniform panels bit-identical across M/U; optimizer call counts 675/101 250/101 250/675/2700 (M) and 0/… (U); label-flow and storage checks all zero failures; B05 tests 15 passed per NOTES (not re-run here).

## 3 Scientific assessment

**Is the decomposition sound?** Γ = Δ_uniform + A_M is an exact identity and the DM uses it honestly. The narrative attached to it — "training-bank gain" versus "greedy deployment cost" — is not supported once F1–F3 are known. Δ_uniform compares two banks trained under almost the same label law (uniform vs uniform-with-tilt-and-AR-correlation) and lands at +0.0354, the same magnitude as the B01→B03 reversal (+0.0197 → −0.0105); with n = 1 it is uninterpretable as a training-regime effect. A_M compares two deployment rules on one bank, one of which (argmax) is a degenerate extraction of a flat policy; its negative sign is expected and recurs in 4/4 banks. Neither term measures a skill combination forming, which is the direction's question.

**What does it say about HMASD's high level here?** Not "undertrained" in the usual sense and not "unnecessary" in an interesting sense: as configured, it is regularised into a uniform sampler (F1) on top of labels that the discriminators cannot recognise (F3). In that regime the hierarchy's only contribution is temporally extended random label switching, which is a known and useful exploration mechanism (see §4) and precisely why uniform deployment does as well as or better than anything learned. The honest statement is: with λ_h = 0.07 and chance-level discriminators, HMASD at fixed N/k reduces to a low-level policy conditioned on random 10-step tokens; whether learned assignment can add value has not been tested yet.

**Converging or wandering?** Protocol discipline is high (prospective endpoints, common initialisation, RNG isolation, identity controls, full adverse-world reporting). Scientifically the direction has cycled through five recipes in one day (pair auxiliary → objective deletion → recurrence → training law → deployment law), each closed by a single-seed result, while the two diagnostics that would have redirected it on day one (coordinator conditional entropy, discriminator log-probability vs chance) sat in every `training.jsonl`. The working explanation has become a ledger of closed recipes rather than a mechanism hypothesis. That is wandering at the hypothesis level with excellent bookkeeping.

**Which pending option has more information value?** Neither, as specified. The 0-fit O/S/R run is nearly free and already implemented, so it should be run — but its expected J outcome (S ≈ R > O) is predictable from F1, and its value is in confirming F2 through label histograms and per-decision entropies from the saved arrays, and in providing the O identity control. A 2-fit M/U replicate at the current λ_h would replicate a near-null comparison and cannot reach the 3-seed minimum anyway. The informative next experiment is the one that removes the entropy pin (§5, R2).

## 4 Literature position

HMASD (Yang, Yang, Lu, Zhou, Li, NeurIPS 2023; from memory, the paper itself is unreachable through this session's proxy) trains a transformer coordinator that autoregressively emits a team skill Z then per-agent skills z_i every k steps, low-level skill-conditioned policies with intrinsic reward λ_e r_env + λ_D log q_D(Z | s) + λ_d log q_d(z_i | o_i), and a high-level policy on the extrinsic k-step return, with entropy terms on both levels; results are on sparse-reward SMAC/Overcooked-style benchmarks with skill-usage visualisations and ablations on team/individual skills and skill counts. This direction reproduces the mechanics faithfully (high-level reward = 10-step env sum ≈ 0.68 [verified]; discriminators; AR decoding) but in a regime where the paper's two premises — distinguishable skills and a sharpened selector — do not hold.

Related lines: HSD (Yang, Borovikov, Zha, AAMAS 2020) uses a discriminator-driven skill layer under a high-level macro policy; ROMA (Wang et al., ICML 2020) learns role embeddings with MI regularisers and reports role-usage clustering; MASER (Jeon et al., ICML 2022) generates subgoals from replay; option-critic (Bacon et al., AAAI 2017) documents option degeneracy without deliberation cost; DIAYN (Eysenbach et al., ICLR 2019) shows discriminator rewards can produce distinguishable-but-useless (often static) skills; HIRO (Nachum et al., NeurIPS 2018) treats the non-stationarity that makes the high level learn slower than the low level; and "Why does hierarchy (sometimes) work so well" (Nachum et al., 2019) finds that much of hierarchy's benefit comes from temporally extended, often random, high-level exploration — the exact effect visible here as uniform ≥ own.

Known failure modes present or unexcluded here: entropy-pinned selector (F1; the mirror image of skill collapse), chance-level discriminator making the MI term a constant bonus (F3), high level learning ~150× fewer steps than the low level (F7), argmax extraction of a stochastic hierarchical policy (F2). Standard diagnostics the field expects and that are absent from all direction records: skill-usage histograms per agent/time/state at deployment, an MI estimate or discriminator accuracy vs chance reported as a skill-distinguishability result, per-skill trajectory or heat-map visualisation, a random-high-level baseline reported as such (U/uniform is one, but it is framed as a deployment rule rather than as the field's null), a fixed-distinct-label baseline, and ≥ 3–5 seeds per arm. The direction's uniform/U control is the right null; it is currently winning.

## 5 Recommendations

Cost unit: 1 fit = 360 k team transitions ≈ 43–78 runner-min on the 4070 in this direction.

**R1 (0 fits, do now).** Run the accepted B05 O/S/R evaluator, and add a zero-cost reading from the saved trajectories: per-panel team/individual label histograms over worlds × renewals, the fraction of decisions where all six agents receive the same label, per-decision entropy from the stored factor log-probabilities, and KL(S-label frequencies ‖ uniform). Also state the coordinator conditional entropies and implied discriminator log q from `training.jsonl` for all nine existing fits. Prediction: O near-constant labels; S ≈ R in J and users; S > O. This converts B05 from a deployment-rule choice into the diagnostic the direction lacks.

**R2 (6 fits).** Selector-sharpness intervention: M with λ_h reduced by 10× (0.07 → 0.007) or with the existing entropy-target mechanism (`hmasd/agent.py:1107-1143`) set to a target of ~0.5 ln 6, 3 seeds, versus 3 seeds of native M, each evaluated under own-sampled, own-argmax and uniform on the fixed 1700200–231 panel. Primary: own-sampled − uniform per seed; secondary: conditional entropy and label histograms. This is the first experiment that can show learned assignment beating random labels; if it cannot at any sharpness, the direction's question is answered negatively with ≥ 3 seeds.

**R3 (0–1 fit).** Skill distinguishability: report discriminator accuracy on held-out transitions against chance and a shuffled-label control from the existing checkpoints (0 fits); if at chance, switch on the already-implemented prior correction (`use_prior_corrected_intrinsic`, which turns log q into log q + ln 6 ≥ 0 at better-than-chance) and re-read B02's question inside R2 rather than as a separate deletion arm. Optionally 1 fit of a "fixed distinct label per UAV" bank to test whether the low level conditions on labels at all.

**Stop.** Further auxiliary heads (P/G), coefficient-deletion arms, the 2-fit M/U replicate at λ_h = 0.07, single-seed recipe cycling, and reporting "k/32 worlds" as evidence strength in RESEARCH rows. Retire the RESEARCH sentence that reads B02 as showing discovery-reward value.

**Publishability.** On its own: not publishable. n = 1 per arm, no positive effect of the learned hierarchy over random labels, discriminators at chance, and the one robust finding (uniform ≥ argmax, 4/4 banks) is a known consequence of an entropy-pinned selector. With R1–R2 done and ≥ 3 seeds it could become a candid negative/diagnostic result ("HMASD's high level contributes nothing beyond random temporally extended labels at fixed N in this UAV task, and why"), workshop-level. As a section of a team-size-transfer paper: in its current form it is harmful, because it shows the component the paper claims transfers is inert; but the uniform/U null is exactly the "random-skill" ablation reviewers of such a paper will demand, and a version in which a sharpened, distinguishable-skill hierarchy beats that null at fixed N is a prerequisite for any claim that it transfers across N.
