## Decision value

R05 asks an answer-changing variable-`k` question rather than merely measuring
a component: can one shared two-UAV policy retain direct physical service and
tail robustness across held-out fixed `k=8` and both registered switch
directions by preparing and applying one certified standby handover?

The frozen panel has unusually high decision value because its admissible
outcomes delete or select concrete mechanism families:

- NEVER and the recovery witness distinguish absence of recoverable headroom
  from learned-policy failure;
- paired REAL/SHAM distinguishes application of the prepared standby from the
  matched transaction shell;
- independently trained IMMEDIATE/HYSTERESIS can delete learned timing if a
  simple deployable rule is sufficient;
- strict-containing FLEX can retain the broader family or show that any
  STRUCTURED advantage is finite-budget restriction/learnability rather than
  expressivity; and
- fixed-versus-switched schedule aggregation can distinguish a fixed-only
  result from common switched-`k` value.

The exhaustive first-match law also makes negative and nonidentifying outcomes
useful: protocol invalidity, competence failure, missing witness/headroom,
support failure, harm, nonactuation package effect, shadow-actuation nonpass,
simple-rule sufficiency, FLEX superiority/nonretention, no-materiality and
unresolved intervals have distinct consequences. A completed valid panel can
therefore simplify, retain or reject the prepared-shadow mechanism without
inventing an interpretation after results.

## Identifiability risk

Identifiability is strong relative to the complexity of the claim, but not
guaranteed:

- literal containment and the REAL/SHAM fork sharply constrain the main
  comparator and transaction-shell alternatives;
- causal tapes, complete-panel retention, independent block clustering and one
  simultaneous max-t family constrain selection and multiplicity shortcuts;
- competence, witness, headroom, precision and support gates prevent a missing
  effect from being called mechanism failure when the package was not
  answerable; and
- package/schedule/stratum atomicity prevents a passing fixed or favorable cell
  from standing in for switched-`k` value.

The remaining risks are material. The accepted-tape rejection distribution is
unmeasured; competence or trigger support may fail; the simultaneous family may
make intervals nonanswering; a pre-onset handover may exhaust the sole
transaction; joint camera missingness may make Mahalanobis overlap permissive;
and finite-budget FLEX training may dominate the interpretation of a
STRUCTURED advantage. These risks are already bounded by the frozen branch and
claim law. They argue for measured Gate A/B construction before a full panel,
not for redefining r05 or declining all construction now.
