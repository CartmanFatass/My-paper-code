## Scientific question

In one prospectively defined fixed-roster-per-episode relational host, can a typed structured prior
reduce the finite sample, update, or compute required to reach a native physical-return threshold on
held-out roster sizes relative to a strictly containing, competent, same-information generic
relational learner? This first object does not exercise within-episode membership change and makes
no variable-membership or churn claim.

The structured and generic arms must receive the same raw entity values, public relations, action
head, environment interactions, minibatch order, optimizer updates, parameter/work budget, and
evaluation opportunities. The comparison is about finite-resource inductive efficiency, not unique
information or a larger reachable policy class.

## Smallest discriminator

Freeze one variable-`N` host with a native action/return endpoint, at least one held-out `N`, and one
held-out relation/field combination. Compare a typed structured arm with a strictly containing
generic learner under a matched work vector. Add an analytic compiler, a relation-reassociation cut,
and a raw-value classifier. The generic learner must first pass competence.

The primary endpoint is held-out native return or regret together with work-to-threshold. A
structured-arm advantage that survives competence and disappears under reassociation supports only a
finite-resource relational inductive bias. Equality, generic superiority, compiler absorption, or
cut-insensitive gain closes the tested mechanism. Support or competence failure is nonidentification.

## Claim ceiling

Even a positive result is limited to the frozen host, resource budget, roster distribution, and
held-out cells. It cannot establish graphon truth, covariance information uniqueness, specialized
credit necessity, general geometric optimality, arbitrary-`N` transfer, churn robustness, UAV
deployment, or safety.
