## Why the changed question is worth one observation

The strongest case against pausing is more specific than “three seeds are too few.” The original positive observation is a real native-return result against two substantive controls. The strongest opposing result shows that blanket erasure is not a dependable default in the observed instances. Together they motivate examining the strength of the state intervention, without assuming that an arrival or departure makes all of a continuing car's history obsolete. A car's own trip continues; some prior local observations may remain useful even when another participant changes. That is a plausible hypothesis about roster change under partial observability, not a demonstrated explanation of either endpoint. [Original scope response, “Decision value and the strongest contrary case”][p78]; [B01 intake, §11][i1]; [B03 intake, §10][i3].

The selected differentiating hypothesis is modest: **a fixed reduction in incoming recurrent-state magnitude at a real public event may yield a better trained policy than leaving that state untouched, without requiring complete clearing.** Half attenuation changes the input to the recurrent update and its learning path while retaining a nonzero dependence on the incoming vector. It does not selectively remove stale teammate information, preserve exactly half the useful information, or mix RETAIN and EVENT policies with equal probability. A GRU is nonlinear; its gates and learned representation may compensate for, amplify or be disrupted by scaling. There is no established monotone relation between the multiplier and return, nor evidence that 0.5 is optimal. This is why the claim must remain a measured complete-package difference. [Proposed option (b)][task]; [FOUNDATIONS, §§2–4][foundations].

My reason for selecting this particular constant is not a fitted optimum or a belief that intermediate parameters must perform intermediately. It is the one already specified, nonzero intervention between unchanged carry and zeroing, with no coefficient search and a clear before-GRU meaning. It tests a concrete alternative action-history rule at essentially the same known learner workload as another pair. The record's positive and adverse observations make that particular change worth a bounded measurement in my judgment; they do not provide an expected effect size or an estimated probability of success.

The strongest contrary case remains serious. The most recent full-clearing loss is large, B02 did not reproduce B01, training aggregates consistently favor retention, and the new multiplier has no observed performance support of its own. Ordinary retention already provides usable generic history without this intervention. Broad rollout spread and only three fitting instances leave open that the observed changes are largely instance-specific. The proposed change may simply add an ineffective or harmful inductive bias. Past spending is not a reason to continue, and a one-line state operation does not make retraining free. These considerations make the author's narrow pause reasonable; I depart from it because one concrete alternative can add a new usable rule, whereas another unchanged pair principally refines the already mixed full-erasure record. [Family question intake, options and recommendation][question]; [B03 intake, §§10–13][i3].

An unchanged fourth pair would still be legitimate replication. It could reduce uncertainty about the full-clearing package and might be preferable if that package's repeatability were itself the priority. I do not reject it for lacking a guaranteed sign, a fixed seed quota or causal localization. For this next-question decision, however, the alternative rule has greater marginal value than another observation of the same extreme intervention. I select neither a sweep nor a sequence of substitutions until a favorable result appears. This use of contrary outcomes to choose one new B is consistent with §§5.2 and 11.8–11.9; those sections permit adaptation but do not make further compute automatic. [Empirical specification][spec].
