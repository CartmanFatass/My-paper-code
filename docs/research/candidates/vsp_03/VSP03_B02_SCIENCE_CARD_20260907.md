## Native host and event-to-credit path

There are two fixed controller/target identities, 0 and 1, and one job per controller. All state is public. There are no joins, departures from the roster, replacements or rejoining agents; a target's occupancy return is not a roster event. Both target processes advance all **40 primitive transitions**, even after a job is submitted, fails or finishes.

Each target starts `y=1,d=0`. At transition t→t+1, an occupied target leaves with probability `1/(d+4)` and an absent target returns with probability `1/2`. The new dwell age is `d+1` only if the previous occupied state stays occupied, otherwise zero, including re-entry. The two exogenous processes are independent. The law and uniform arithmetic follow the existing NumPy binary64 path; learner tensors are float32.

A public fair bit assigns one identity clocks `0,4,...,32` and the other `2,6,...,30` for the whole episode. At a clock, an actual CONTINUE/SUBMIT decision exists only if that identity's job is pending and the single slot is free. SUBMIT is irreversible, pays one attempt cost and occupies the next eight transitions. There is no queue, cancellation, retry, collision lottery or early release after a failed service sample. At a t+8 boundary, complete the earlier service sample, settle service and free the slot **before** the next decision. A decision cannot see future samples `y(t+1),...,y(t+8)`.

CONTINUE reserves nothing: the partner may submit at its own clock two ticks later. A busy slot forces waiting and produces no sampled action or policy-gradient row. A submission at t=26 therefore removes a pending phase-zero partner's t=28 and t=32 opportunities. This is a consequence of the selected law, not evidence that yielding is better. The task is coupled but remains fully public and describable as centralized scheduling.

A submitted job succeeds iff its eight service samples are all occupied. It contributes integer units `200*success - 10*attempt - waiting_ticks`; waiting is the number of primitive ticks before submission, or 40 if never submitted. Count the phase-two identity's first two ticks and waiting after its final clock. The joint terminal return is both jobs' units summed and divided by **400**. No shaping, rule agreement reward, per-decision normalization or post-deadline waiting deletion is allowed.

For the interval [s,s+1), charge 10 units for a submission accepted at boundary s; charge one waiting unit per job still unsubmitted after that boundary's action; credit 200 units for a successful service completing at s+1. If this sum is `u_s`, team return is `sum(s=0..39,u_s)/400`. The actor's target at decision t is `sum(s=t..39,u_s)/400`, equivalently final units minus actual rewards already accrued before t, divided by 400. Primitive gamma=1; terminal bootstrap=0. Partner consequences remain through t=40, even after the acting controller's service finishes. N1's `(terminal_units+decision_time)/200` shortcut is invalid for this team reward.

## Public features, policy and references

Each target has the shared public event processor `a,e`, initially zero. At a pending controller's clock, read current features first; actual or forced CONTINUE then sets `a=y,e=0`. A departure while armed latches e through re-entry. SUBMIT clears that job's bits; later clocks do not rearm a submitted job. Readiness is always recomputed as `b=a*y*(1-e)`, including for the partner, rather than cached from an earlier decision.

Every valid T/G actor and critic input is the same 14-vector, in this order: `t/40`; own `(y,d/40,a,e,b)`; partner `(y,d/40,a,e,b)`; partner pending bit; own phase bit (0 for clocks 0,4,...; 1 for clocks 2,6,...); partner's next remaining fixed clock time divided by 40, with 1 when none remains. The clock is public calendar information, not a prediction that the future slot or target will be available. No future tape, realized future success, privileged critic state or T-only veto is supplied.

Each arm has one shared actor `14→32→32→1` with tanh hidden layers and a trainable direct own-b coefficient, plus one shared critic `14→32→1`. Both controllers call their arm's same parameters in their own/partner perspective. Actor/critic/total parameter counts are **1570/513/2083 per arm**. Corresponding hidden and critic initialization is paired between arms. Actor output residual weights start at zero. G output bias/direct-b start at zero; T starts at `-log(3)` and `2*log(3)`. All parameters then train. T/G differ only in this initialization intervention, not policy class or information.

R is a fixed same-information rule: at a legal opportunity submit iff own b=1, except yield if the partner is pending, has a remaining future clock, currently has b=1 and has **strictly greater** current dwell age. Ties submit now. No extra exception for the acting job's last clock is inserted. R0 submits iff own b=1 at a legal opportunity, with no partner-yield condition. R0 is T's initial greedy rule by the chosen coefficients; it runs directly as a rule, with no extra model or training. Both respect the real occupied slot. Neither is an oracle or tuned baseline.

## Learner and prospective RNG realization

For each arm, collect 128 batches of 128 complete joint episodes with parameters fixed within each batch. Perform exactly one joint backward and Adam step per batch: lr=0.001, betas=(0.9,0.999), epsilon=1e-8, weight_decay=0. Actor loss sums valid log-probability×detached-advantage rows across both controllers and divides by the number of complete joint episodes. Critic loss is 0.5 times mean squared error over valid rows. Entropy is summed over valid rows per joint episode with coefficient `0.01*max(0,(64-u)/63)` for update u=1,...,128. No padding/forced-wait/post-submit row becomes a gradient sample. There is no replay, hidden epoch, clipping, reward/advantage normalization or hyperparameter search.

The following ordinary RNG realization is fixed before any model or output, within the Pro-selected independent B02 namespace:
