### 1.2 Strongest simpler explanation of the S7 pattern

**DERIVATION — the pattern is instrument-limited, not opportunity-limited.** Three facts combine:

1. *Small ordinary increments on the remaining decision objects.* Every measured layout/scheduling/scoring increment beyond a competent planner is ≤ .06 QoS/step (R−G +.029; O−P +.063; P_BS−S0_BS +.039; 5/10-step +.016). A learned increment over the same object cannot plausibly exceed the ordinary one by much; its expected size is inside .00–.06.
2. *Resolution of one training instance.* The instance/checkpoint spread on this host is ≈ .05–.12 (b05 B vs SET at matched checkpoints −.03 … +.12, `NOTES.md:6739, 6791` [V]); the S7 seed SD is unmeasured and the replication attempt ended in two technical failures (`RESEARCH.md:1006`, `energy_relay_baselines` row [V]). A single-instance reading of a .03–.06 effect is uninterpretable by the method's own statistics (`SKILL.md:272–285`).
3. *Optimisers that did not move.* Each macro-cadence learner was initialised at (or near) the ordinary choice and trained on 3,400–6,400 macro decisions of an H3000 team return; the critics' explained variance stayed ≈ 0 and the clip fraction 0 (table above). With a per-decision effect of order .01–.05 QoS against a 3000-step team return, the advantage signal-to-noise per sample is tiny [I]; nonactivation (L ≡ P, L ≡ R) is the *modal* outcome of that design, independently of whether a better policy exists. The one from-scratch per-step learner (SET) reaches .44–.48 at 1.2 M and is still improving (`NOTES.md:6809` [V]) — ordinary finite-learning incompetence, the critic's simpler alternative (`successor_selection_20260928/REVIEW.md:46` [V]).

The competing explanation "the host has no learnable headroom" predicts exactly the same observations. The five studies therefore do not discriminate between the two; that is the finding. It is consistent with Root's synthesis ("neither study identifies a dominant … bottleneck; neither establishes a useful learned increment", `archive/2026-09-28/RESEARCH-native-round3-synthesis.md:35–37` [V]) and with the 09-26 diagnosis's instrument layer (`docs/Claude_docs/reviews/RESEARCH_PROGRAM_DIAGNOSIS_AND_RESET_20260926.md:40–49` [V]).

**Where the large effects are.** The only effects ≥ .10 measured on S7-S2 are information-structure effects: H_central−H_local .177 (`NOTES.md:2505–2520`), S0_BS−H_BS +.107 and P_BS−H_BS +.146 (`uav_information_value/NOTES.md:1434–1435`). They were recovered by ordinary priors and anchors, not by learning — and H_local is a legal-observation-pooling central controller, not a decentralised one (`NOTES.md:508, 785` [V]), so there is no communication gap left to learn on this host without an artificial bandwidth constraint.

### 1.3 What would change the reading

- A decision object on S7-S2 with a *predicted* effect ≥ .10 — none of W′, O, or Root's two angles predicts one (the only measured layout increment over geometric targets is R−G ≈ +.03, `REVIEW.md:54` [V]; a learned offset much beyond it would be surprising [C]).
- n ≥ 3 training instances of one learner with a within-instance paired reading. This is affordable on the macro-cadence recipes (persistent_service fit wall 27.76 min, `uav_persistent_service/NOTES.md:747`; joint_transition 120.4 min runner incl. O, `uav_joint_transition/NOTES.md:698`; active_sensing 89.6 min, `RESEARCH.md:999` [V]) and not on SET (0.027–0.034 s/transition → 9.1–11.3 h per 1.2 M, `successor_selection_20260928/MEMO.md:66` [V]; two faults per ≈ 1.37 M steps in this direction, `NOTES.md:6714`, plus two failed fits at 432 k/858 k in `energy_relay_baselines` (`RESEARCH.md:1006`) → ≈ 4 faults in ≈ 2.7 M recorded CUDA training steps; DERIVATION: ≈ 1.8 expected faults per 1.2 M fit, survivable with the resume contract at ≈ 0.6–0.8 h each).
- A macro-decision learner whose critic reaches explained variance materially above 0 — none has.

### 1.4 Is S7-S2 still a host where a MARL cooperative-planning question can be decided at one-fit cost?

**No, for learned-over-planner increments** (expected ≤ .06, band .05–.12, modal outcome nonactivation or a flat instance). **Yes, only** for (i) a question with a predicted effect ≥ .10, or (ii) a question run at n ≥ 3 on a recipe costing ≤ 2 h per fit, with the ordinary policy as a *candidate* rather than the initialisation and a within-instance paired reading. The 10× cost asymmetry between the macro recipes and SET is the lever the brief does not name: on S7-S2 the right unit of a learned comparison is three cheap instances, not one expensive one.
