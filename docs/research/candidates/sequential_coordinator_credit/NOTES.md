**Question.** For a hierarchical MARL coordinator that assigns skill labels to agents in a fixed order (HMASD's autoregressive SkillCoordinator: z_1, …, z_K sampled with prefix conditioning) and is trained on one shared k-step team return, which per-agent learning signal lets it learn non-additive role structures — relay chains where the team's service depends on the *joint* presence of a relay and its servers — and in which regimes (redundancy of roles, strength of chain binding, reward noise) does each signal win or fail?

**Contribution sentence if fully successful.** "For fixed-order skill coordinators with non-additive (chain-type) team rewards, we give a regime map showing where the exact removal difference credit D_i = R − R(−i) beats the learnability-optimal prefix-conditional baseline (COSAC's Sequential Aristocrat Utility) and where it collapses (role redundancy), and we extend the sequential aristocrat baseline from the one-step bandit to k-step skill segments executed by a learned, non-stationary low level." The map is the shared insight: the relay host (`energy_relay_benchmark`) measures its operating point (relay-hop share, D sparsity) and reads the method to use on its T-prime substrate.

**Host (stage A, controllable, exact ground truth).** `chain_bandit`: K agents (K = 4 and 8) choose labels in fixed order from {IDLE, RELAY, SERVE_1 … SERVE_C} (C = 2 or 3) with prefix visibility (agent i sees x and z_<i), softmax policies with a shared trunk as in HMASD's decoder (tabular or small linear per agent). Team reward R = Σ_c min(d_c, access_c, backhaul_c) + ε, with access_c = Σ_{i: z_i = SERVE_c} q_ic (capped by d_c, so doubling a server beyond demand adds nothing — the redundancy dial), backhaul_c = B if any agent chose RELAY else b_0 (the chain-binding dial is B − b_0; a stronger AND variant requires a RELAY among agents whose mask allows it), ε Gaussian noise (the noise dial). ## 2026-09-27 — Correction after the peer's independent review (Root, commit f3d7cd49b; the same review reused, no new grid): the "D carries no gradient under AND coupling" claim is withdrawn

**Claim withdrawn.** The neighbours entry above (D++ paragraph) says that under AND coupling "D is exactly zero for every agent while the coalition is incomplete … so D carries **no gradient** there" and that "the first cell's exact-gradient reading will show this directly (D's expected update vanishes on those contexts)". The second half is false as a general proposition. The reviewer's counterexample is accepted and verified: two independent Bernoulli agents with logit parameters, R = a1·a2, removal to idle (c_i = 0) gives D1 = R − 0 = R on every sample, so the expected logit gradient under D1 equals the one under R, p1(1−p1)p2 > 0 (numerically at p1 = .3, p2 = .6: E[D1·∂ log π1] = E[R·∂ log π1] = .1260). Zero credit on the sampled joint actions where the coalition is incomplete is not zero expected gradient: the complete-coalition samples carry R to every pivotal member.

**What is true, restated.** Under pure AND coupling with removal-to-idle, D coincides with the shared return on every sample (every present member is pivotal), so D loses its *advantage* over the shared return — no variance reduction and no per-agent separation — not its gradient. Under m-of-K over-completeness, D gives 0 to the non-pivotal (redundant) members and R to the pivotal ones; that is the intended shaping. In the chain host's binding configuration, D_i is the chain mass that i's presence enables; on samples where the chain is incomplete every member's D is 0, and the expected gradient is what the policy's sampling of complete chains makes it. D++'s m = 6 result (G and D learn nothing, D++ learns) is a sparsity-of-pivotal-samples failure under a coevolutionary learner's random exploration (complete 6-coalitions are rare, so both fitness signals are almost always 0), which D++'s added copies remove; it says nothing about the expected policy gradient. The "third named mechanism" recorded in the D++ paragraph is therefore retitled from "D's expected update vanishes under AND" to "D degenerates to the shared return under AND coupling (no advantage) and to a sparse signal when pivotal configurations are rare under exploration". The first cell's primary reading measures D's gradient bias and per-sample variance against the exact ∇J at the three snapshots and reports them; it predicts nothing about a vanishing update. Three items stay separate, as the reviewer asks: the fixed-order suffix dependence through z_{>i} (COSAC's indirect effect; the first cell reads it as the D-vs-oracle-D gap with z_{>i} resampled), the joint normalisation of advantages (the predeclared triple calibration), and PPO clipping (not modelled in the first cell; a stated omission of the microhost → HMASD transfer).

**Chen, Li, Liu, Yang, Gao — MAPPG (AAAI-23, pp. 11542–11550).** Object: the *centralized-decentralized mismatch* — in the multi-agent stochastic policy-gradient theorem (eq. 2, p. 11544) agent a's gradient for u_a is scaled by the marginal joint value M_a(s, u_a, π_{−a}) = Σ_{u_{−a}} π_{−a}(u_{−a}) Q(s, u), so sub-optimal teammates can make M_a(u_a*) < M_a(u_a^#) although Q(s, u*) > Q(s, u^#). Method: replace Q by a *polarized* joint value Q^{PPG}(s, u) = (1/β) exp(α (Q(s, u) − Q(s, u_curr))) with u_curr the current greedy joint action (eq. 3–5, p. 11544) inside the same gradient (eq. 6); Theorem 1 (p. 11545): for α above log π_{−a}(u*_{−a}) / (Q(s, u^sec) − Q(s, u*)) every agent's polarized marginal value is maximised at its component of u* (optimality consistency, under a unique optimal joint action); Theorem 2: joint policy improvement to π*. Practice (eq. 8, p. 11546): pessimistic bound over two target critics, zeroed gradients when Q̂^{PPG} < 1 or π_a(u_a) > P ≥ .5. Evidence: QTRAN's matrix game (only MAPPG reaches 15, Table 1 p. 11547), the MTQ differential game, eight SMAC maps against COMA / MADDPG / DOP / FOP / FACMAC (Fig. 3, p. 11548). *Reading for this direction.* It is a simultaneous-action, shared-signal method: all agents receive the same nonlinearly reweighted joint value, i.e. a monotone shaping of E1's signal toward the greedy joint action, with a learned critic; not a per-agent difference or prefix-conditional credit, and it assumes a unique optimum. The mismatch it names is the same dilution that the fixed-order decomposition (Kuba Lemma 1; SeqAU's prefix conditioning) treats by conditioning on the realised prefix instead of averaging over π_{−a}. Its expected gradient is biased away from ∇J by construction (the exp reweighting), which the first cell's exact-gradient reading would register as bias. Not an arm of the first cell (arms are fixed by Revision 1); recorded as the nearest *shared-signal shaping* alternative, cheap to add later on the bandit if a positive credit result needs a "same information, nonlinear shared signal" control. Label: RECORDED, not NEW.

**Liang, Wu, Wang, Cai — Asynchronous Credit Assignment (IJCAI-25, pp. 170–178).** Object: agents whose actions take different numbers of steps (Fig. 1, p. 170: waiting / discarding / padding all mis-assign credit). VSP (Def. 1, p. 172): at each step agents split into decision-makers i_d and executing agents i_c; each executing agent gets a virtual proxy i'_c with the same policy re-executing the ongoing action, so credit at that step reaches the policies of both; Theorem 1 (p. 173): VSP preserves the Markov perfect equilibrium and the convergence of value-decomposition operators. MVD (eq. 5, p. 173): Q_tot = k_0 + Σ k_i Q_i + Σ_{i_d, i'_c} k_{i_d i'_c} Q_{i_d} Q_{i'_c} — a multiplicative interaction between a current decision and the already-committed actions of others, called their *conditional dependency*; Theorem 2: the additive class is a strict subset of the multiplicative one; IGM required only over decision-makers (eq. 7); monotone implementation with |f| weights and a Q^min shift (eq. 8, p. 174); K-th order interactions (eq. 6) degrade (Fig. 7b, p. 176). Benchmarks: asynchronous SMAC, Overcooked, POAC (five seeds). *Reading for this direction.* Value-based CTDE, not a policy-gradient credit, so not an arm. What transfers is the named mechanism: a decision's value depends multiplicatively on commitments others are still executing. HMASD re-decides all labels synchronously every k steps, so the coordinator has no VSP-type asynchrony; the mechanism appears at Stage B (k-step segments with a learned low level), where a label's payoff depends on the skills the others are mid-way through. Label: RECORDED for Stage B.
