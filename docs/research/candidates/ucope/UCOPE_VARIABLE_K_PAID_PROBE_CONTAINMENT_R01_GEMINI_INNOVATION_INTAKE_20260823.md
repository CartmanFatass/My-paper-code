## EM intake

1. **Permutation sample efficiency — accepted as an attribution diagnostic.**
   A count summary explicitly supplies permutation invariance that RAW must
   learn. This is a plausible mechanism of finite-budget advantage, not a
   violation of equal information. Revision 02 adds a no-retraining
   `RAW-PERMAVG` forced-tail diagnostic. If it contains COUNT, any positive
   COUNT-over-RAW result is attributed to explicit invariance engineering, not
   broad raw-history insufficiency.
2. **Held-out-k interpolation — accepted.** Revision 02 evaluates the same final
   checkpoints on both train-k and held-out-k populations. A gap present only
   on held-out k receives an interpolation-specific claim ceiling.
3. **Early SHORT oversampling — rejected for this object.** Every batch is
   exactly regime-balanced, so the first 5% of training, an integer number of
   complete batches, cannot contain the proposed regime-count imbalance.
4. **RAW gradient variance — retained as the strongest finite-budget
   alternative, not repaired in-place.** A different optimizer or batch size
   would be a new treatment and is prohibited after the frozen run. The claim
   remains limited to the stated learner/budget.
5. **Action-conditional regime decay — deferred to a new object.** It changes
   the transition law and is appropriate only if R01 earns a dynamic successor.
6. **Stochastic UAV costs — deferred to a new object.** R01 explicitly claims
   only deterministic-cost local value; hardware-cost robustness is outside its
   ceiling.

No Gemini proposal adds a panel, learner, run, seed, runtime, implementation,
lease or UAV claim to the primary R01 acquisition gate. The two accepted
