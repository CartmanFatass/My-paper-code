## Intake conclusion

The review supplies one decisive algebraic correction and several useful prospective controls. The correction narrows how the already-running B1 may be interpreted; it does not change B1's treatment, arms, worlds, budget, activity boundary, execution, or technical acceptance.

At every post-reset event in the B1 card, `keep(a)=1-switch(a)` because every survivor either retains or changes its prior role, including dummy. Therefore

`score_B(a) = bid(a) + coverage(a) + 0.04*keep(a)`  
`             = bid(a) + reward(a) + 0.04`.

The final term is assignment-independent and cancels from both the temperature-one softmax and greedy argmax. Candidate B is consequently a learned-bid perturbation of a reward-complete, switch-cost-aware joint decoder. `B-REBIND - A-JOINT` identifies the finite-budget effect of inserting the known switch-cost component into the already coverage-aware decoder. It does not identify learned persistent binding or learned event-to-event rebinding.

B1 remains scientifically useful as a prespecified end-to-end package and inductive-bias experiment. Its corrected maximum B-specific claim is narrower than the original card: a positive B contrast may support a switch-cost-aware reward-coded joint allocation package on this constructed host, not a learned persistence mechanism.

## Accepted scientific corrections

1. `A-MASS - G-MEAN` jointly changes the sum of agent embeddings and analytic pressure ratios. It can support a finite-budget benefit from the explicit extensive-feature bundle, but cannot attribute that benefit specifically to the pressure ratio.
2. In `CAPACITY_NORMALIZED`, `sum_i c_i[r]/d[r]=1.35`, so every `rho[r]=log(1/1.35)`. Any A advantage there cannot be caused by variation in the pressure channels.
3. Task-wise mass is not sufficient for allocability. Equal-`N`, equal-demand, equal-task-mass rosters may differ because one-role-per-agent feasibility depends on capability fragmentation and cross-task competition.
4. Static `N={4,6}` is held out from training but lies inside the trained cardinality range `{3,5,7}`. It supports held-out-size interpolation, not beyond-range cardinality extrapolation.
5. At `t=0`, B and A-JOINT use the same decoder score. A static B difference is training-induced package transfer and is not direct rebinding evidence.
6. A zero-bid B decoder would greedily maximize the current segment reward relative to its own history, modulo reward-equivalent ties. A prospective `B-ZERO-BID` control is therefore the most direct test of whether learned bids add value beyond the reward-complete decoder.
7. A future generic comparator should have ordinary sum-capable set aggregation or task-conditioned attention and the same prior-role access. This separates analytic mass calibration from a deliberately mean-only computational bottleneck.
8. A future persistence treatment must use an adaptive lease, invalidation gate, or reduced-cost release decision and must preserve service in switch-required cases. Merely rewarding fewer switches is not enough.
9. A common scalable, reward-blind allocator should enter the identifying experiment rather than being deferred until after an exact-enumerator result.

These corrections are adopted as interpretation and successor-design constraints. The proposed Residual-Demand Repair Auction is a prospective hypothesis, not evidence: preserve useful survivor bindings, compute task deficits, score agent-task repairs with shared learned bids, and coordinate them through bounded auction or dual prices without inserting realized coverage, waste, or switch reward into the decoder score.
