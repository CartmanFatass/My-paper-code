Yes. Reading the whole record, the most useful ideas are about where the learning question is placed and how it is priced, not about another recipe. Six ideas, ranked by consequence per cost. All are advice only, since the Claude session stays paused.

1. **Measure a learning curve before buying any more single-exposure fits.** Nearly every learner study in the repo ends at one exposure of one to three CPU-hours with a band verdict. That design cannot tell "unlearnable" from "underexposed". Cheapest act: on the coupled host, label worlds with the planner's own static winner (about one second per world, no H500 rollouts), train the same distillation student at 1k, 4k and 16k worlds, and read dev regret against log data. A flat curve points at the representation. A falling curve says the sizing rule itself is the binding constraint and shows the price of a competent learner. Roughly three to five CPU-hours, and it would change the compute policy, which affects every other direction.
2. **Put the MARL claim where a centralized planner is not a lawful competitor.** The only retained learned-over-ordinary gains in fresh worlds are communication content (the B/O/L assets against C) and the S1 learned banks. Reframe HMASD's hierarchy as a bit-budgeted protocol: the team skill is a six-way broadcast every k ticks, about 2.6 bits, and individual skills are per-agent codes. Compare it against ordinary codebooks at the same bit budget, such as a cluster-index broadcast, under contention or bandwidth limits. The message-content and contention directions already hold assets. This is the one framing in which "hierarchy" does a job a planner cannot do.
3. **Give HMASD's own mechanism the kind of task it was built for.** Discriminator-driven skill discovery exists for sparse-reward exploration.
