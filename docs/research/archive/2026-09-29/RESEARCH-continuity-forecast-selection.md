**Recommendation**
Select both revised exploratory comparisons: service at `4a188a330`, and content at `029b36574` with correction `192eacd23`. Neither is an unchanged extension of the closed study. If only one marginal investment is affordable, prioritize content: it offers a more direct learning-versus-ordinary-prediction comparison at lower execution and engineering cost.

This was a separate-context review, not a blinded one: the assignment included prospective summaries. I reconstructed original protocols and consequential evidence before reading the complete proposed explanations and earlier reviews.

**Evidence**
R has a substantial finite access benefit worth preserving. On B04, R improves mean full/late QoS over O_H by approximately `.0621/.1066` and removes the observed overload, cutoff and depletion failures. But worlds 04/08 lose on all four principal endpoints, and R06 introduces a 55-step terminal service outage. B03’s six-member cohorts still consume `1010.94 W` against `1000 W` charging capacity. These observations support neither sustainability nor unchanged-R expansion.

The evidence also rejects treating all-F as an established outage diagnosis. In R06, 172 of 312 late zero-service steps occur outside all-F; R08 has 505 all-F steps with no zero-service overlap. A service-aware scheduling hypothesis remains plausible without presuming that fixing all-F fixes service.

Content’s earlier studies establish a competent **learned geometry baseline**, not the futility of communication or prediction. B02’s L-minus-B native J differences change sign across continuations: approximately `[-.0225, +.0954, -.0290]`. B03 selects frozen B19451 by its declared point-mean rule, while retaining adverse worlds. More evaluation of those fixed assets would not answer the new forecasting question.

I directly rechecked hashes and native endpoints for service R01/04/06/08 and consequential positive/adverse content pairs, and inspected channel/tick-order source. I reused applicable independent complete-stream, configuration and exogenous-binding audits rather than reconstructing every old artifact again. No new implementation, runtime estimate or admission has been verified.

**Service Comparison**
The enduring question is whether ordinary joint replenishment scheduling can preserve lawful access while improving service continuity. It does not need learning novelty.

The initial substantive objection was compulsory transfer priority: preserving every eligible R transfer would remove an important **when-to-depart** choice. Root’s correction, now incorporated, is consequential. S can defer/service, while forecasting lost access slack, energy deadlines and native F consequences. Preserving R’s capability no longer means preserving its trigger.

The final design also forecasts the relevant coupling: 30 steps is the replanning cadence, not the predictive horizon. The horizon extends to 1,200 steps and includes travel, priority-based charging, inherited commitments, timeout/full release, F hysteresis and outbound deployment before readiness.

The strongest constructive prediction is:
- Lower-service-cost departure and better-timed replenishment change joint availability.
- Those changes reduce overlapping deployment losses or accelerate service recovery.
- Native full/late QoS, J and service gaps improve over frozen R.

The strongest simpler alternative is that current-geometry marginal service costs plus an additive availability approximation do not predict the consequential radio dynamics. Moving users, interacting transmitters, approximate travel and the retained arrival mismatch can defeat this package. These are falsifiable risks, not reasons to require an exact simulator or a positive preliminary test.

The smallest worthwhile complete observation is the declared **all-16-world comparison**, with 192,000 new S steps and exact frozen-R reuse. Both panels matter: one contains capacity-deficit configurations and the other contains access benefits and terminal-service failures. These are development-exposed worlds, not independent confirmation.

The finite-use rule is meaningful: native continuity improvement cannot purchase new cutoff, depletion or terminal-zero worlds. Passing supports bounded usefulness on these panels, not sustainability. Proxy improvement without native improvement rejects the proposed translation; unchanged choices indicate limited exposure; an active adverse intervention counts against adoption. None automatically requires a repair or another run.

**Content Comparison**
The corrected question is about **fallible, dated future-position forecasts**, not committed future control. B19451 has no target, hold or timer to announce.

G/O/F is a substantive comparison. It preserves geometry competence, matches continued motion learning, and gives F a qualified ordinary competitor. O uses the sampled first action followed by the actor’s central command, rather than extending one noisy action throughout the horizon. F must add useful predictive information beyond that ordinary baseline.

The timing claim is supported under the retained channel: delivery precedes action selection, RR sends each member every five steps, delays are one or five, and there is no modeled packet loss. Cached age is therefore at most nine; the dated ten-step endpoint remains future during in-horizon use, including terminal truncation. This conclusion would not survive arbitrary loss, queueing or changed scheduling.

The packet change is genuine: all primary arms use 40 bytes rather than the old 28. Their comparison is matched under the model’s unchanged fee/delay assumptions; comparison with frozen B0 is cross-contract. It cannot establish free additional bandwidth in a physical network.

Detached predictor training avoids an auxiliary-gradient explanation for changing the actor representation. It does not prove that any native benefit is caused specifically by forecast accuracy: F may provide a useful learned summary of private state. The proposed accuracy and immediate-response diagnostics strengthen interpretation without becoming mediation evidence.

The smallest worthwhile observation is the declared **three arms × three conditional continuations**, their complete fresh panels, and frozen B0:
- F improving native J/service over both O and G supports this conditional learned-forecast package.
- O improving over G without F’s increment favors ordinary prediction and ends the need for this learned recipe.
- Better forecast error without native benefit rejects usefulness in this comparison.
- All continuations losing to B0 implicates adaptation or the richer contract; mixed continuations remain mixed evidence.

These are three continuations from one learned parent, not three independent training replications.

**Cost and Disposition**
Service costs zero fits but an estimated **3–5 worker-CPU hours plus 5–8 support hours**. Content costs **nine policy fits, three trained predictors, 1,261,568 native steps**, and an estimated **35–50 minutes of scientific runtime plus 4–6 support hours**. Predictor work is correctly counted separately. Engineering effort, particularly for service, is a substantial opportunity cost.

Remote-first execution is appropriate; Root’s reported peer allocation does not establish node exhaustion. Shared-machine timing and actual overhead remain uncertain. Fresh runner admission, published implementation inputs and focused engineering checks remain required, without another scientific review round.

The literature provides useful constructions, not host guarantees: [fixed-site charging scheduling](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.12656), [Intention Sharing’s original review version](https://openreview.net/references/pdf?id=k71zbVCDy), and [DACOM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.01619). I did not verify final-version differences for Intention Sharing.

Stopping both would leave two decision-relevant alternatives untested: ordinary scheduling versus R’s timing, and learned prediction versus competent ordinary prediction. Their bounded costs justify these purchases, without creating an obligation to expand either afterward. Existing pauses, old verdicts and peer-owned investments remain unchanged.
