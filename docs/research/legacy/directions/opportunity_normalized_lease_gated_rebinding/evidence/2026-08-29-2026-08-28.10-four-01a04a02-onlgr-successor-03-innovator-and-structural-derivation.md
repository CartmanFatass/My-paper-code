## EM-independent derivation

For spacing `Delta`, let `K` be the geometric number of real opportunities
between renewal events, with event probability `q`. A regenerative cycle has
physical length `Delta*K`, receives service for
`min(Delta*K,8)` ticks because renewal resets a nonstacking eight-tick service
lifetime, and pays one cost of `2`. Since `8/Delta` is an integer,

`E[min(K,8/Delta)] = [1-(1-q)^(8/Delta)]/q`.

Dividing expected cycle reward by expected physical cycle length gives the
frozen identity

`J_Delta(q) = 1-(1-q)^(8/Delta)-2q/Delta`,

with `J_Delta(0)=0` by the long-run limit. Premature renewals are included:
they discard remaining service lifetime and pay another cost. The fixed-q
payload-null shell has zero long-run service and return `-2q/Delta`; its gap
from the real renewal policy is exactly the policy's service rate. The shell
is a valid payload check but is algebraically redundant once the policy beats
KEEP.

  `3479236575/4294967296`, cost `3/8`, and direct return
  `1868623839/4294967296`, approximately `0.435072891`.
- `SPARSE`, `Delta=4`: unique unrestricted grid optimum `q=12/16`, service

  `{10/16,11/16,12/16,13/16,14/16}`. Their minimum separation is

- Both stratum optima beat KEEP by much more than `1/32`; their matched-shell

The two frozen deployed families are structurally distinct. Their probability
vectors are `(p,p)` and `(r,1-(1-r)^4)`. Equality forces
`r=1-(1-r)^4`, whose only solutions on `[0,1]` are the degenerate endpoints
`0` and `1`.

## Decisive simpler physical-rate explanation

The raw event-probability heterogeneity does not imply two physical operating
rates. Convert each unrestricted optimum to executed events per physical tick:

- `DENSE`: `(3/16)/1 = 3/16`;
- `SPARSE`: `(12/16)/4 = 3/16`.

They are exactly equal. The one-parameter simple family

`q_Delta(h)=min(1,Delta*h)`, with `h in Q`,

selects `h=3/16` and reproduces both unrestricted stratum optima exactly. Its
equal-stratum return is the unrestricted ceiling
`4284542943/8589934592`, approximately `0.4987864456`. Its advantage over the
best exponential exposure family is only
`148279329/8589934592`, approximately `0.0172619858`, below the frozen
`1/32` material margin.

This is the smallest answer-changing result. The construction establishes
that one constant probability per opportunity is poor on unequal partitions,
but it does not establish a need for the exponential cumulative-survival
link. The apparent optimal-probability heterogeneity collapses to one global
executed physical event rate. The exponential family is a competent smooth
approximation to that rate on this object, not a uniquely identified
mechanism.

The service clock makes this unsurprising: the host axiomatizes eight ticks of
physical service, so cumulative physical-exposure survival is the aligned
positive control. An opportunity-aged service law would instead align the
diagonal per-opportunity family. The host therefore demonstrates consequences
of a chosen clock; it does not discover which clock governs a target task.
