## Why the MSE-exact-reference roots cannot substitute

The six retained exact-reference roots solve a different problem:

$$
\beta_{\mathrm{tail,MSE}}^*
=
\operatorname{lstsq}(X_{\mathrm{tail}},y_{\mathrm{tail}})
$$

followed by:

$$
y_{\mathrm{root,MSE}}^*
=
\operatorname{root\_targets\_fp32}
(B_{\mathrm{root}},\beta_{\mathrm{tail,MSE}}^*)
$$

and then:

$$
\beta_{\mathrm{root,MSE}}^*
=
\operatorname{lstsq}
(X_{\mathrm{root}},y_{\mathrm{root,MSE}}^*).
$$

The required live exact roots instead use either the retained `THREE-WITNESS` learned tail or the retained `DOSE-MATCHED-SINGLE` learned tail. Since the tail vector is an input to every PROBE root target, the two exact-root problems generally have different target arrays and different optima.

The MSE reference may therefore be used only as a **deterministic reconstruction check**. It cannot be used as:

* either live arm’s exact root;
* a substitute treatment or comparator;
* evidence that a live arm’s induced target is root-safe; or
* evidence about finite-step residual error.

Both the dissent and the source make this distinction explicit.
